Abstract
Let G be a finite simple graph of order n with adjacency eigenvalues λ1 (G) ≥ · · · ≥ λn (G), and write S3 (G) =
λ1 (G) + λ2 (G) + λ3 (G) and M3 (n) = max|V (G)|=n S3 (G). The first result of this paper is an all-rank finite identity:
for every 1 ≤ k ≤ n, maximizing the Ky Fan k-sum over simple graphs is exactly equivalent to maximizing the
entrywise positive part of a rank-k orthogonal projection. Equivalently, if ηmix (k; n) denotes the finite mixed projection
functional, then Mk (n) = n2 ηmix (k; n) − k. For k = 3 we derive an explicit closed-wheel (pole–pentagon) variational
family and prove that its unique interior optimum has coefficient

second-order carrier reductions. The second is a computerassisted compact upper-bound programme. Some legacy

all-order equality or uniqueness classification.

The third spectral sum admits a cleaner structure than
a direct graph census suggests. The graph maximization can be performed exactly inside the Ky Fan variational principle, producing the finite identity Mk (n) =
nηmix (k; n)/2 − k at every rank. At rank three the closed
wheel reduces the candidate extremal geometry to one
concave variable and yields the exact pole–pentagon coefficient α3 . The remaining obstruction is not a vague search
over all graphs: the all-tail Hessian has a range/gain
factorization, two-dimensional closure banks admit exact
area inequalities, induced paths span the full normalization tangent, the minimal 2 + 2 carrier is globally
eliminated, and higher signed frame support compresses
to the two irreducible sectors (2, 3) and (3, 3).
This is a substantial narrowing of the S3 problem while
preserving the line between an unconditional theorem
and a certificate-dependent conclusion. A complete release now has a precise target: finish the replay-complete
compact upper certificate and close the residual highersupport incidence/stationarity sectors. If those gates
are discharged, the same document immediately yields
M3 (n) ≤ α3 n − 3 and ηmix (3) = 2α3 with no change to
the unconditional derivations.
