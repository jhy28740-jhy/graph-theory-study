\chapter{The graphon variational problem}\label{ch-variational}\label{sec-variational}

The bound of Theorem~\ref{main} is proved by passing from graphs to graphons,
solving an attained variational problem there, and transferring the answer
back. This chapter carries out the first two steps. Section~\ref{sec-notation}
and Chapter~\ref{ch-prelim} have fixed the meaning of $\Sigma_3(W)$ and
$\mathcal M=\sup_W\Sigma_3(W)$; here we establish that $\mathcal M$ is
attained, locate it between two explicit constants, and extract from
maximality every structural consequence that the later chapters consume.

The reason for working with graphons rather than with graphs of growing order
is that $\Lambda_3$ is not continuous in any elementary graph metric, whereas
$\Sigma_3$ is continuous in the cut metric by~\eqref{cut-eigen}. Attainment
then supplies an object to which calculus can be applied: a maximiser has a
canonical eigenframe, and perturbing that frame inside the space of graphons
produces exact first- and second-order identities. Those identities, and not
any counting argument on finite graphs, are what make the structural results
of this chapter and of Chapter~\ref{ch-support} possible.

Two warnings about the logical order of the argument. First, no eigenvalue
simplicity is assumed at the outset; the relations $\lambda_1>\lambda_2$ and
$\lambda_3>\lambda_4$ are consequences, and they are derived in
Lemma~\ref{ellipse-structure} and Lemma~\ref{contact-structure} by comparing
genuinely different orthonormal frames. Second, the lower bound
$\cstar\le\mathcal M$ is proved by an ordinary quotient computation for a
join and is independent of the priority question discussed in
Remark~\ref{rem-newton}.
